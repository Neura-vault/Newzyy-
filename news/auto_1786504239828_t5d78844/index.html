Meta faces expensive child safety reckoning

By Newzyy Staff — 4d ago

Meta is facing escalating legal and financial consequences over concerns about child safety on its platforms, with recent court rulings signaling a turning point for social media companies and their responsibilities to young users.

The pressure on Meta has intensified after a New Mexico court ordered the company to pay $567 million into a fund aimed at addressing teen mental health issues linked to its platforms. This follows a $375 million judgment in March, where a jury found Meta negligently allowed harm to children’s mental health and concealed evidence of child sexual exploitation on its services. In a separate Los Angeles case, Meta was ordered to pay $4.2 million for designing platforms that allegedly addict and harm children, a ruling that has since inspired hundreds of similar lawsuits.

The company now faces 42 lawsuits from state attorneys general, school districts, and families, with thousands more cases pending against Meta and other platforms like YouTube, TikTok, and Snap. The central issue in these cases is whether social media companies have contributed to rising mental health crises among youth through addictive design features that may worsen depression, anxiety, and suicidal tendencies.

Plaintiffs are seeking not only financial penalties but also structural changes to these platforms, including stricter age verification, usage limits, and prominent safety warnings. Legal experts suggest the wave of litigation reflects a growing recognition that tech companies cannot evade accountability for the harm their products may cause.

As part of the New Mexico ruling, Meta has been directed to enhance its age verification tools, which currently rely on AI to assess factors such as the ages of users’ connections and the content they engage with. The court also mandated the development of a predictive model to identify potential risks to young users, signaling a potential shift toward more proactive safety measures.
