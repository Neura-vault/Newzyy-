‘I remember going: oh hello!’ Dara Ó Briain on comedy, coolness, cruelty and finding his birth parents

By Newzyy Staff — 1h ago

Dara Ó Briain, the Irish comedian and host of the popular satirical panel show "Mock the Week," is known for his quick wit and ability to keep audiences entertained. As he prepares for a new season of the show on TLC, he reflects on its legacy and the challenges of navigating comedy in a politically charged landscape.

Arriving at the studio, Ó Briain is already in character, playfully suggesting interview questions that match the comedic photoshoot. His charm and sense of humor are evident as he ditches his jacket and leads the way to a quiet corner in a nearby pub.

"Mock the Week," which ran for an impressive 17 years on BBC Two, has become a platform for up-and-coming comedians to showcase their talents. Ó Briain describes it as a "debutante's ball," offering a chance for those who have made a name for themselves at the Edinburgh Festival to transition to screen.

The show's political leanings have been a topic of discussion, with accusations of left-wing bias. Ó Briain, who identifies as "centre left," attributes this perception to the political climate during the show's run, with a succession of Conservative Prime Ministers providing ample material for satire. However, he acknowledges that the show may have leaned left, with a lack of representation from the right-wing perspective in British comedy.

Ó Briain suggests that the direction of comedy punches matters, and audiences have a threshold for what they find acceptable. He cites examples of jokes about nurses and their pay, which audiences may not resonate with, and the challenge of finding the right balance between humor and sensitivity.

The show has also faced criticism for its male-dominated and allegedly vicious nature. Ó Briain admits that the criticism is valid, especially considering the show's early years when such a culture was more prevalent in similar panel shows. He expresses relief at not having to participate as a contestant, finding the competitive nature of the show too intense for his liking.
