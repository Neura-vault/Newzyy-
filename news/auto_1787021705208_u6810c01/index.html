Reform’s Tice accused of misinformation after urging people to enjoy heatwaves

By Newzyy Staff — 53m ago

Reform UK’s deputy leader Richard Tice has faced criticism for remarks dismissing climate action and urging people to embrace heatwaves, with opponents accusing him of spreading misinformation.

Speaking at a press conference on Monday, Tice argued that efforts to address the climate crisis were misguided, claiming there was no evidence that net zero policies would halt climate change. He suggested that adapting to warming temperatures was more practical than attempting to prevent them, adding that Britons should appreciate benefits such as improved English wine production. “When it’s a bit warm, let’s enjoy it,” he said. “And if it means that English wine and sparkling wine gets better and better, fantastic.”

His comments drew sharp rebukes from political and medical figures. Labour’s Bridget Phillipson described his remarks as “tone-deaf,” highlighting the risks posed by wildfires, droughts, and threats to food and water supplies. She questioned his priorities, given the severe impacts of recent extreme heat.

Tice’s statements followed a summer marked by record-breaking temperatures, with the UK experiencing multiple days above 35C. The heatwaves contributed to thousands of deaths, widespread hospital admissions, and an estimated £4 billion loss in economic output. Government data indicates that 2,877 people died during May and June heatwaves—twice the total from the previous summer.

Health professionals have also raised concerns, noting that older adults are affected by heat at lower temperatures than previously believed. The Royal College of Nursing criticized Tice’s remarks, stating that those urging people to “enjoy” climate change should consider the reality facing hospitals, where staff are overwhelmed and patients suffer in extreme conditions.

Medical experts warn that climate change is worsening public health emergencies, with vulnerable groups particularly at risk. The criticism of Tice reflects broader debates over how to respond to rising temperatures and their far-reaching consequences.
