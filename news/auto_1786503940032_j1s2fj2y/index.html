Terry Lewis obituary

By Newzyy Staff — 4d ago

Terry Lewis, the former Labour Member of Parliament for Worsley in Greater Manchester, has died at the age of 90. He served 22 years in the House of Commons, establishing a significant record as a dissenting voice against his party’s frontbench policies on numerous left-wing issues.

Elected in 1983, a period when the Labour party's left-wing influence was diminishing, Mr. Lewis spent much of his parliamentary career in opposition to his own leadership. He was a familiar figure on the backbenches, often alongside fellow MPs Dennis Skinner and Bob Cryer, forming what was informally known as the "awkward squad," challenging both Conservative governments and his own party's direction.

Mr. Lewis consistently appeared in lists of Labour's most rebellious MPs. Between 1992 and 1997, he voted against party whips on 49 occasions. Following Tony Blair's election as Prime Minister, he temporarily held the record as the most frequent rebel.

After leaving Westminster in 2005, citing disappointment with the Labour government's commitment to socialist principles, he publicly resigned from the party five years later by tearing up his membership card, stating the organisation had "lost its soul." He later rejoined in 2015 when Jeremy Corbyn became leader.

Mr. Lewis’s political convictions were deeply rooted in the left-wing political and trade union culture of post-war urban Manchester, a stance from which he never deviated. A devoted Manchester United supporter, he was widely popular in his constituency, known for his unwavering commitment to the causes he championed, earning him the affectionate nickname "El Tel" and a reputation as a Salford legend.

Born in Ordsall, near Salford docks where his father, Andrew Lewis, worked, he was the son of Mary (nee Redfern) and Andrew Lewis. He received his education at Our Lady of Mount Carmel Roman Catholic primary and secondary schools in Salford. He won the newly established Worsley seat in 1983, securing over 40% of the vote. Upon his retirement, he sent a postcard to local contacts captioned “Into the Sunset,” with the message “Still searching for the socialist alternative.”
