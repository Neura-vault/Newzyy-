Reform politicians are such a joke, they deserve a slot on the Edinburgh fringe | John Crace

By Newzyy Staff — 1h ago

Reform UK, once a tightly controlled vehicle for Nigel Farage’s political ambitions, has devolved into a chaotic spectacle that even its own members struggle to take seriously. What began as a disciplined operation—where MPs acknowledged their dependence on Farage—has unraveled into a farcical display of infighting and financial irregularities.

The party’s recent attempt to present itself as a credible alternative to the Conservatives backfired spectacularly with the announcement of a “shadow cabinet” consisting of just four figures: Robert Jenrick, Suella Braverman, Richard Tice, and Zia Yusuf. The selection drew immediate ridicule, with critics questioning whether any of them could plausibly lead a government department. Yusuf, in particular, faces widespread skepticism, not least because he has never been elected as an MP and is widely disliked within the party. His inclusion appears to stem from financial contributions rather than political competence.

Financial controversies have further eroded Reform’s credibility. A £5 million donation from crypto billionaire Christopher Harborne remains unexplained, raising questions about its origins and purpose. Additional funds linked to individuals under investigation for money laundering have added to the party’s troubles, undermining its self-styled image as a champion of the people.

Farage’s decision to call a by-election in Clacton—ostensibly to challenge the political establishment—only highlighted the party’s decline. The campaign’s focus on a man dressed as a bin drew widespread mockery, exposing the lack of substance behind the spectacle. With Farage now largely absent from the public eye, Reform’s internal divisions have come to the fore, culminating in the suspension of prominent member Tim Montgomerie. Once a loyalist who genuinely believed in the party’s mission, Montgomerie fell victim to Farage’s intolerance for dissent, revealing the hollowness of claims about fostering open debate within Reform.
