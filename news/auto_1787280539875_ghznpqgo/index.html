Ministers are in denial about the scale of the crises we face | Letter

By Newzyy Staff — 17m ago

Government officials continue to underestimate the severity of the climate and ecological crises, according to a recent letter highlighting the lack of resources dedicated to climate adaptation. Despite repeated warnings from experts, only 20 out of 6,600 staff in the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (Defra) are reportedly working on climate adaptation measures, even as the country grapples with droughts, wildfires, and extreme heat.

Families have faced disruptions such as school closures and hospitals postponing appointments, while wildfires have caused widespread damage. Official figures indicate that more than 2,700 deaths have been linked to extreme heat so far this year. The letter argues that the government’s response remains inadequate, despite consistent calls from the Climate Change Committee for stronger action.

The authors question whether the impacts of climate change and ecosystem collapse have been deprioritized by policymakers. They point to recent controversies, including delays in decisions on new oil and gas projects, as evidence of inconsistent leadership. The letter also criticizes the absence of a dedicated minister for resilience and adaptation, despite the presence of other specialized roles in government.

Critics argue that climate adaptation requires a coordinated, cross-departmental approach rather than being confined to a single desk within Defra. They call for a dedicated commissioner to oversee preparations for climate risks and ensure communities receive clear guidance and protection. The letter concludes by questioning why these concerns have not been addressed in government discussions.
