John Hall obituary

By Newzyy Staff — 1h ago

John Hall, a pioneering British test pilot whose career spanned the dawn of supersonic aviation and the golden age of commercial flight, has died at the age of 98.

Born in East Bridgford, Nottinghamshire, Hall developed an early fascination with aviation during World War II, when he watched Spitfires soar overhead as a boy. His dream of flying fighters became reality in 1946 when, at 18, he enlisted in the RAF and trained at Cranwell. By the early 1950s, he was instructing pilots on Meteor jet bombers before joining the elite team behind the English Electric Lightning—the RAF’s first and only fully British-built supersonic interceptor.

As a test pilot in the late 1950s, Hall pushed the Lightning to its absolute limits in some of the most demanding trials of the Cold War era. His endurance test in freezing conditions stands out as one of the most harrowing: flying at 36,000 feet, his aircraft became encased in ice, rendering his hands nearly useless and stiffening the controls. After 90 minutes aloft in agony, he managed a rough landing, his body barely able to move as ground crew extracted him from the cockpit. Hall also survived a structural stress test in which the recoil from the Lightning’s three 30mm cannons—fired simultaneously—produced a deafening blast and lit up the cockpit with warning lights, yet the airframe held firm.

After leaving the RAF, Hall transitioned to civilian aviation, flying Boeing 707s for British Caledonian Airlines on transatlantic routes for 25 years. His career bridged two eras of flight: from the raw power of experimental jets to the routine of commercial travel.

Hall is survived by his second wife, Tessa, and four sons—James, Patrick, Richard, and Maxwell—from his two marriages. He leaves behind a legacy as a fearless aviator whose skill and resilience helped shape Britain’s aerospace history.
