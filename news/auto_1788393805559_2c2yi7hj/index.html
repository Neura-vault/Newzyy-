Patagonia sues Trump over slashing of protected areas in Utah

By Newzyy Staff — 1h ago

Outdoor apparel brand Patagonia has filed a lawsuit against former President Donald Trump over his decision to shrink two national monuments in Utah, arguing the move violated federal law.

The company’s legal challenge targets Trump’s 2017 proclamations, which reduced Bears Ears National Monument by about 85% and Grand Staircase-Escalante by roughly 50%, stripping protections from nearly 3 million acres. Environmental groups and tribal nations have long opposed the reductions, citing the cultural and ecological significance of the land.

Trump justified the rollback by stating the areas contained valuable minerals and could be used for energy development to support national security and resource independence. His administration framed the decision as a way to balance conservation with economic use.

Patagonia’s lawsuit contends that the president overstepped his authority by disregarding legal requirements for monument designations. The company has previously criticized the move, calling it an attack on public lands and Indigenous heritage.

The case adds to ongoing legal battles over the fate of the monuments, with several tribes and conservation organizations also challenging the reductions in court. The outcome could influence future federal land management decisions.
