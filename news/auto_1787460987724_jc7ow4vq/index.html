The Guide #257: What we lose when a medium ​built for the ear surrender​s to the camera

By Newzyy Staff — 6m ago

This year has marked a definitive shift in podcasting, as the medium—historically rooted in audio—fully embraces video. While the transition has been gradual, with early examples like Joe Rogan’s webcam recordings in 2009, 2026 has seen the transformation accelerate into a near-universal standard. Major platforms have led the charge: Netflix began broadcasting video versions of popular podcasts from The Ringer, iHeartMedia, Barstool, and others in January, while continuing to produce original video podcasts of its own.

YouTube’s dominance in this space has driven much of the change. After becoming the top podcast platform in the U.S. in 2024, YouTube achieved the same in the U.K. by 2026, overtaking Spotify. In response, Spotify has expanded its own video podcast features as part of a broader effort to retain users within its ecosystem. Meanwhile, podcasters are increasingly adopting video production setups, often repurposing studio spaces with furniture ill-suited for conversation—think felt throw pillows and chairs that force awkward swivels between guest and camera.

Not everyone welcomes this shift. For many listeners, podcasts serve as a screen-free companion during tasks like ironing, commuting, or exercising. The addition of video disrupts that simplicity, making the experience feel more performative. Some hosts, despite their talent for audio, struggle to adapt to the camera’s demands, risking a dilution of what made their podcasts unique in the first place.

Yet resistance may be futile. The video pivot offers clear advantages, particularly for smaller podcasts with limited marketing resources. Clipping standout moments for social media can attract new audiences, a strategy that has become essential in a crowded digital landscape. Some podcasts even thrive visually, especially those featuring comedians who naturally engage with an audience.

While purists may lament the loss of podcasting’s original charm, the medium’s evolution appears irreversible. The challenge now is balancing the benefits of video with the preservation of what made podcasts so valued in the first place.
