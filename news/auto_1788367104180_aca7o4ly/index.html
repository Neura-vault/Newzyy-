Fake 10 Downing Street listing exposes 'unfit' Booking.com, says consumer group

By Newzyy Staff — 1h ago

Booking.com has defended its platform after a consumer advocacy group exposed a fraudulent listing claiming to be located at 10 Downing Street, the official residence of the UK Prime Minister.

The incident was highlighted by a consumer rights organization, which argued that the fake listing demonstrated systemic failures in Booking.com’s verification processes. The group described the platform as "unfit" to host legitimate accommodations, citing the ease with which misleading listings can appear.

In response, Booking.com acknowledged the issue but emphasized that the misleading entry was part of a "limited test" conducted by the consumer group. The company stated that the test did not accurately represent the experience of its millions of real listings or genuine user reviews.

Booking.com added that it continuously works to improve its verification systems to prevent fraudulent or inaccurate listings from being published. The company did not provide further details on specific measures currently in place.

The consumer group has called for stricter oversight of online accommodation platforms, urging regulators to intervene and ensure greater accountability for platforms hosting such listings. The incident raises broader concerns about the reliability of user-generated content on travel booking sites.
