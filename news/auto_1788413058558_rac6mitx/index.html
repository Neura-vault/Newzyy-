Ink review – Danny Boyle turns the birth of the Sun into a lurid tabloid frenzy

By Newzyy Staff — 54m ago

The Venice Film Festival has witnessed the premiere of Danny Boyle's latest film, "Ink," an explosive and unapologetic portrayal of the birth of a tabloid giant.

Starring Jack O'Connell as the formidable editor Larry Lamb, the film is a gripping adaptation of James Graham's 2017 play, shedding light on the untold story of Rupert Murdoch's acquisition of The Sun newspaper.

Boyle's direction is bold and unsubtle, employing a unique visual style with Dutch angles and exaggerated close-ups, capturing the frenzied energy of the tabloid world. The film is a visceral experience, filled with violence and a raw portrayal of the media industry's darker side.

Guy Pearce delivers a compelling performance as the enigmatic Rupert Murdoch, showcasing his transformative skills as he leads the charge in revolutionizing the newspaper industry. The film's focus on the power dynamics within the media empire is a fascinating insight into the prehistory of modern media.

"Ink" paints a picture of a bygone era, where the upper echelons of Fleet Street enjoyed lavish meals and suits, in stark contrast to the gritty, ink-stained reality of the journalists and print workers. This dichotomy adds a layer of complexity to the narrative, highlighting the class divide and the sacrifices made in the pursuit of power and influence.

While the film may not appeal to those accustomed to the glitz and glamour of modern-day media depictions, it offers a raw and authentic glimpse into a world that shaped the media landscape as we know it today.
