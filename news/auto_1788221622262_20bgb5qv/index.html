Unlike needy Keir, trash-talking Andy speaks a language Trump understands | John Crace

By Newzyy Staff — 52m ago

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham has taken a markedly different approach to relations with Donald Trump compared to his predecessor, Keir Starmer, signaling a shift in how the UK may engage with the US president in the future.

Starmer, who previously sought to cultivate a close relationship with Trump, found himself repeatedly rebuffed by the US leader, who appeared to view the UK’s overtures with indifference. Despite Starmer’s efforts—including offering a state visit and emphasizing the “special relationship”—Trump’s actions, such as the recent bombing of Iran, left the former prime minister appearing hesitant and reluctant to respond. Critics, including figures like Kemi Badenoch and Nigel Farage, urged a stronger stance, but Starmer’s approach ultimately yielded little in return.

Burnham, by contrast, has adopted a more direct and unapologetic tone. As mayor of Greater Manchester, he had previously declared Trump unwelcome in the city, a stance that resonated with many in the UK frustrated by what they saw as excessive deference to the US president. Now, as prime minister, Burnham has shown no intention of softening his position ahead of a potential meeting with Trump at the UN General Assembly in New York later this month.

Burnham’s approach reflects a pragmatic stance, emphasizing that while the UK will engage with Trump when necessary, there will be no false displays of friendship. He has dismissed the need for apologies, noting that Trump’s own rhetoric—including dismissive remarks about Manchester—has long been part of the political landscape. Burnham’s strategy appears to prioritize the UK’s interests without the performative diplomacy that characterized Starmer’s tenure.

In essence, Burnham’s approach suggests a more transactional relationship with Trump, one where the UK engages on practical matters without expecting personal warmth or long-term alignment. The upcoming meeting in New York may set the tone for how this dynamic unfolds in the months ahead.
