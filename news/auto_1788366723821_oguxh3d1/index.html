Whisky galore – and now plenty more besides: exploring the Scottish island of Islay

By Newzyy Staff — 1h ago

Islay’s economy and tourism sector are increasingly shaped by its distilleries, which have long been central to the island’s identity. Their success has created opportunities for a wider range of businesses, including breweries, farms, restaurants, and chocolatiers, all contributing to Islay’s growing appeal as a destination.

Cycling along the shores of Loch Indaal, a rider can watch the Paps of Jura fade into the mist as rain clouds gather overhead. Seeking shelter, one might reach Port Charlotte youth hostel, where a quick change of clothes precedes a visit to Lochindaal Seafood Kitchen. Just before the rain arrives, the restaurant offers a warm welcome and a steaming bowl of creamy fish chowder.

The promise of fresh seafood proves irresistible, leading to an order of the seafood platter. Though intended for one, the generous portion includes fat langoustines and crab claws, hot whisky-butter scallops, and fresh bread. The meal arrives just as the downpour begins, turning a simple stop into a satisfying refuge.
